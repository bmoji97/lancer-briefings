## Extract
The Lost Horizons Far Field Team is one of the Union Science Bureau's premier expeditionary research organizations operating beyond the established frontiers of Union space. Unlike the USB's more academically focused divisions, Far Field Teams are designed to accompany major expeditions into poorly understood regions, providing scientific expertise during first-contact scenarios, archaeological surveys, xenoecological studies, technological investigations, and anomalous phenomena. Though officially a civilian research body, the Lost Horizons Team maintains extensive logistical and engineering assets that allow it to operate independently for years at a time.

Serving as the expedition's headquarters is the USV *Far Horizons*, an immense long-range exploration vessel designed less as a laboratory and more as a mobile scientific institute. Unlike military flagships, nearly every deck aboard the *Far Horizons* serves a research function. Modular laboratories, fabrication bays, botanical preserves, geological repositories, astronomical observatories, clean rooms, archaeological vaults, fabrication foundries, and dedicated blink-space sensor arrays allow the ship to investigate almost any phenomenon encountered during exploration. Entire sections of the vessel remain reconfigurable, enabling new laboratories to be constructed while underway as research priorities evolve.

For nearly two centuries, the Lost Horizons Far Field Team has operated throughout Union space and beyond, accompanying colonial expeditions into uncharted systems and investigating locations considered too remote or dangerous for conventional academic institutions. Their work has resulted in dozens of new stellar surveys, the recovery of forgotten colonial archives, countless ecological discoveries, and several classified findings whose existence remains known only to the USB itself.

## Command Structure
Unlike either the Union Navy or the Department of Justice and Human Rights, the Lost Horizons Far Field Team is led by a single director. Overall authority belongs to Expeditionary Director Dr. Cassian Rourke, a veteran expedition leader whose career has been defined by long-duration exploration missions far beyond established Union territory. He is advised by rests the Directorate, a council of senior researchers representing the expedition's principal fields of study, including astrophysics, xenoecology, archaeology, engineering, anthropology, applied mathematics, blink-space physics, and paracausal systems research.

Officially, Dr. Rourke serves as a member of the Intervention Fleet Task Force alongside Admiral Julus Morrone and Director-General Helena Varga. In practice, however, the USB enjoys autonomy. Whenever matters fall within the realm of scientific investigation, Lost Horizons personnel answer directly to the Dr. Rourke rather than military or civilian authorities. This independence has occasionally created tension with both the Navy and the DOJ/HR, particularly when scientific objectives conflict with operational priorities.

## Operational Capacity
The Lost Horizons Far Field Team possesses one of the most diverse collections of scientific assets ever assembled for a frontier expedition. Its personnel include astrophysicists, archaeologists, xenobiologists, ecologists, engineers, linguists, anthropologists, climatologists, materials scientists, artificial intelligence specialists, forensic analysts, and experimental physicists drawn from institutions across Union space. Although not a military organization, Lost Horizons maintains a sizeable security contingent composed primarily of Rangers and Bureau Security Specialists tasked with protecting research personnel and recovering sensitive scientific material. In addition, several mech squads assigned to the expedition have been modified specifically for hazardous exploration, operating in environments too unstable or dangerous for conventional scientific teams.

Unusual for a Far Field team, supporting these researchers are dedicated survey flotillas, orbital observatories, autonomous drone swarms, atmospheric sampling craft, deep-core drilling platforms, fabrication vessels, mobile laboratories, and long-range reconnaissance ships capable of operating independently across multiple star systems. The Team's engineering detachments frequently work alongside DOJ/HR reconstruction crews, while its environmental specialists advise both military and civilian authorities on minimizing ecological damage during combat operations.

The expedition numbers approximately twenty-five thousand personnel, nearly half of whom are researchers or technical specialists. The remainder consists of engineers, logistics crews, medical staff, fabrication technicians, pilots, security personnel, and support workers necessary to sustain long-term scientific operations on the frontier. While small compared to either the 32nd Fleet or the Sierra Madre 21st Critical Assistance Mission, the Lost Horizons Far Field Team possesses a level of scientific expertise unmatched anywhere else in the Intervention Fleet.

## Rumors
The team's sudden inclusion in the Huygens Intervention has puzzled nearly every member of the Joint Task Force. Publicly, the USB claims that the deployment is intended to document the environmental and technological consequences of the conflict while assisting reconstruction through geological surveys, infrastructure analysis, and ecological restoration. Official statements describe the mission as a routine scientific attachment to a Critical Assistance Mission.

Few within the Intervention Fleet believe that explanation.

Even among the senior leadership of the Union Navy and the Department of Justice and Human Rights, only a handful of officers have been informed why Lost Horizons specifically requested assignment to Huygens. The USB has declined to elaborate, citing classifications well beyond the clearance of most personnel. Additionally, the inclusion of expeditionary director Dr. Cassius Rourke has turned heads. Although widely respected as one of the USB's foremost coordinators of multidisciplinary research, Dr. Rourke has earned an equally formidable reputation for aggressively pursuing scientific opportunities that many would consider politically inconvenient or operationally hazardous. Rumors have spread throughout the fleet ranging from the discovery of dormant NHP infrastructure to evidence of pre-collapse civilizations or unidentified blink-space phenomena somewhere within the system. Many suspect the war is simply the Bureau's opportunity to investigate something far older—and far more significant—hidden somewhere within the system.
