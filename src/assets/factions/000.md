## Extract
The 32nd Fleet is the third of Union Navy's peacekeeping formations assigned to the Sierra Madre Line, operating primarily from Cerro Gordo Station. While officially designated a fleet, the 32nd only has its supercapital ship and a host of battleships on a permanent basis. Instead, when deployed, it is built around a vast network of auxiliary logistics vessels, escort groups, transport flotillas, repair ships, hospital ships, engineering tenders, and civilian support craft capable of sustaining prolonged  campaigns far from the Galactic Core.

Like much of the Union Navy in the wake of ThirdComm's uprising, it primarily acts in an administrative capacity and is only deployed in times of dire crisis. Instead, auxiliary forces based in their home systems are left to handle local issues.To that end, for more than three and a half centuries, the 32nd Fleet has remained operating on a skeleton crew. The Sierra Madre Line experienced only scattered piracy, isolated colonial disputes, and routine peacekeeping operations, leaving the fleet to focus on disaster relief, infrastructure support, administrative duties, and large-scale logistical exercises.  While critics within Union occasionally dismissed the 32nd as an oversized logistics command masquerading as a combat fleet, few organizations could match its experience in coordinating massive interstellar relief efforts or establishing functioning governments in the aftermath of catastrophe.

Its centerpiece is the fleet carrier UNS-CV *Calamity Jane*, a centuries-old supercarrier that serves as both fleet flagship and mobile command center. Though aging by Cradle's standards, the *Calamity Jane* has undergone continual modernization over the centuries, transforming it into less of a frontline assault carrier and more into a strategic coordination platform capable of directing hundreds of simultaneous operations across an entire star system. During major deployments, its cavernous hangars house not only naval aerospace wings, but also Department of Justice and Human Rights detachments, relief organizations, engineering corps, and Union Science Bureau expeditions.

## Command Structure
Rather than relying on a rigid hierarchy, operational authority typically rests with the Commanders' Congress. Every captain commanding an independent vessel—as well as each commodore responsible for a task group—holds a seat within the Congress. Strategic objectives and logistical needs are debated collectively before being formalized into fleet directives issued under the authority of the Admiral.

This unusual system evolved during centuries of dispersed frontier operations where communications delays and independent deployments made centralized micromanagement impractical. Captains became accustomed to exercising broad autonomy while coordinating with peers rather than waiting for direct orders. The Congress preserves this culture, encouraging initiative while maintaining unified strategic goals.

At its head is Admiral Julus Morrone, a veteran administrator and fleet coordinator respected less for battlefield heroics than for his ability to build consensus among hundreds of strong-willed commanders. Morrone's greatest strength lies in orchestrating immensely complex operations involving military forces, civilian agencies, humanitarian organizations, and colonial governments simultaneously. Though some officers view his collaborative leadership as cautious, few deny that the 32nd Fleet functions with remarkable cohesion despite its decentralized command philosophy. As the designated leader of the 32nd fleet, Admiral Morrone sits on the Intervention Fleet task force, representing the Union Navy.

## Operational Capacity
Contrary to popular perception, the fleet often appears less as an invading armada than as an immense logistical ecosystem. Entire squadrons exist solely to manufacture replacement parts, process refugees, establish orbital hospitals, or construct temporary spaceports in liberated territory. Every successful planetary offensive depends upon the invisible labor of thousands of sailors, engineers, quartermasters, and civilian specialists working far behind the front lines.

That is not to say that the fleet lacks in fire power. Aside from its flagship and permanent battleships, the auxiliary fleet adds 4 capital ships, 13 battleships, and numerous line ships along with their support groups. Behind its might, the 32nd fleet possesses a crew numbering over five hundred thousand strong, with a fifth of that crewing the *Calamity Jane* alone.
